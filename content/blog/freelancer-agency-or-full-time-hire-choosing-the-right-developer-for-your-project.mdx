The right developer depends less on talent and more on fit. A startup building an MVP needs something different from a company maintaining a ten-year-old platform. Here's how the three options compare, from someone who has worked full-time on a remote team and now freelances.

**Freelance senior engineer**

- Best for: MVPs, new features, rescues and well-defined projects
- Time to start: days
- Who does the work: the person you talked to
- Flexibility: scale up or stop anytime
- Overhead: low

**Agency**

- Best for: large builds that need many roles at once
- Time to start: weeks
- Who does the work: often a team you didn't meet
- Flexibility: locked into the contract scope
- Overhead: medium to high

**Full-time hire**

- Best for: a long-term core product
- Time to start: 1 to 3 months
- Who does the work: your employee
- Flexibility: hard to reverse
- Overhead: salary, benefits and equipment

### When a freelancer is the right call

- You need an MVP or a new feature shipped in weeks
- Your team is stuck on a specific hard problem, like real-time data or performance
- You want a senior engineer without a senior salary commitment
- You need someone who can design the interface and build it

### When an agency makes more sense

If you need a designer, three developers, QA and a project manager at once, an agency packages that. You pay for the coordination, and you may not control who works on your code.

### When to hire full-time

When the product is your company's core and needs daily ownership for years, hire. Many clients start with a freelancer to build version one, then hire once the product proves itself.

### How to make a freelance engagement work

1. Start with a small paid milestone to test fit
2. Agree on how and when updates happen
3. Keep code in your own repository from day one
4. Ask for documentation so the next engineer can pick it up
